STUDY DESIGN
THE QUESTION

Can SMILES-derived descriptors predict the three molecular PC-SAFT parameters closely enough to reproduce pure-component saturation properties?

One row = one molecule. At prediction time, only molecular structure is provided. The fitted PC-SAFT parameters m, σ, and ε/k are the supervised labels. FeOs then calculates vapor pressure and saturated-liquid density from the predicted parameters.

Reference quantities use published fitted PC-SAFT parameters. This project measures fidelity to that parameterization, not accuracy against experimental measurements.

04 / SCIENTIFIC FINDINGS

Better parameter MAE.
Not always better VLE.

A useful reminder that optimizing a surrogate target is not the same as optimizing the physical property of interest.

FINDING 01

Complexity did not guarantee downstream accuracy.

On the 39-molecule development-validation set, Extra Trees with 156 features achieved better errors for individual PC-SAFT parameters, yet worse saturation-pressure agreement than the simple Ridge model.

Extra Trees also showed large training–validation parameter-error gaps. This is evidence of overfitting or high model variance, but does not, by itself, establish why its pressure errors were higher.

The molecular parameters interact through PC-SAFT. Smaller errors on three separate targets do not necessarily yield a better thermodynamic state prediction.

FINDING 02

Parameter sensitivity gives a physical explanation to investigate.

Local finite-difference calculations on five development molecules suggest saturation pressure is especially sensitive to the dispersion energy and segment number. Opposing parameter errors may cancel, while reinforcing errors can amplify pressure deviations.

We treat this as a physically motivated interpretation, not a proof of the complete mechanism for large parameter deviations.

WHAT WE CONCLUDE

Predictive accuracy must be measured where the physics happens.

Extra Trees reduced individual-parameter cross-validation MAE with richer features, but the simple five-descriptor Ridge model produced lower development saturation-pressure error. The final, frozen Ridge model achieved 20.54% pressure APE and 2.66% density APE against reference PC-SAFT on 39 untouched molecules.

This supports a narrow, defensible conclusion: optimizing individual parameter labels alone is an incomplete proxy for thermodynamic accuracy. Differences may reflect sensitivity and compensating errors; those mechanisms are not conclusively isolated.

06 / METHODS & REPRODUCIBILITY

What the model knows.
And what it doesn't.

01

Source & selection

FeOs Esper2023 published parameter records, curated to 194 saturated, nonpolar, C/H-only, single-fragment molecules (C3–C20). Invalid records, unsupported EOS terms and duplicate molecular identities are excluded.

02

Preprocessing without leakage

SMILES → RDKit molecular weight, carbon count, ring count, rotatable-bond count and branch points. Median imputation and standardization occur inside a scikit-learn Pipeline, fitted using training molecules only.

03

Baselines & alternatives

Compare mean prediction (DummyRegressor) with Ridge α=1.0. Development experiments also compared Random Forest, Extra Trees and feature spaces of 5, 12, 28 and 156. The final model is the frozen Ridge + 5 configuration.

04

Split & metrics

Fixed molecule-wise 155/39 train/test split (seed 42). Three-fold cross-validation and an internal 116/39 development split guided model selection. Parameter MAE/RMSE and pressure/density APE are reported; 39 test molecules were evaluated once.

05

Thermodynamic calculation

FeOs PC-SAFT receives predicted m, σ, ε/k. Reference and ML states share four temperatures (0.65–0.80 of the reference Tc). Convergence, molecular outliers and property errors are recorded for every attempted condition.

06

Limits & data integrity

Agreement is with published-parameter PC-SAFT, not experiment. Reference Tc sets the temperature grid. The small hydrocarbon dataset and random molecule split permit chemically similar train/test molecules. No mixtures or polar/associating terms.
